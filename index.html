About Me

I am an Electrical & Electronics Engineer whose interests have evolved — steadily and deliberately — from embedded systems and power electronics toward semiconductor devices, nanoelectronics and fabrication. That shift was not accidental.

The turning point was my Erasmus Exchange at Grenoble-INP Phelma, one of Europe's foremost institutions for semiconductor science and microsystems engineering. Studying Advanced CMOS, Semiconductor Physics, Nanoelectronics, VLSI and Microsystems, and working on an MTJ-based random number generator, I encountered the physics and engineering that operates at the boundary of what is currently possible. I became increasingly interested not only in how devices behave, but in how they are designed, fabricated, characterised and optimised — and what it takes to do that reliably at industrial scale.

My background includes 2 peer-reviewed IEEE publications, industrial experience at Robert Bosch and Schneider Electric, a B.Tech in EEE from Amrita Vishwa Vidyapeetham, and ongoing professional work in database architecture and process automation. The common thread across all of it is a preference for rigorous, documented, reproducible engineering.

I am now working systematically toward graduate study in semiconductor engineering and device fabrication, with the longer-term goal of contributing to — and eventually building — a vertically integrated semiconductor manufacturing capability.

Featured Spintronics  ·  MTJ  ·  Numerical Simulation

MTJ-Based Stochastic Random Number Generator

Grenoble-INP Phelma  ·  2025  ·  Research project during Erasmus exchange

Developed a numerical solver for the Landau–Lifshitz–Gilbert (LLG) equation using Heun's second-order Runge-Kutta method, modelling the stochastic switching behaviour of a Magnetic Tunnel Junction as the physical basis for a true random number generator.

The initial implementation did not reproduce physically correct switching distributions when incorporating effective and thermal field terms. I worked through the formulation systematically — studying the numerical method in the literature, tracing where the stochastic field was applied incorrectly in the time-stepping algorithm, and correcting the implementation until the simulation converged to expected behaviour.

This project was my direct introduction to nanoscale magnetic devices and spintronics. It raised questions I could not answer without understanding how such devices are actually made — junction area, barrier thickness, magnetic anisotropy — which deepened my interest in device-level fabrication and thin-film deposition processes.

Featured  ·  2× IEEE Published FEM  ·  Machine Learning  ·  IPMSM Best Paper Award

IPMSM Demagnetization Fault Prediction via FEM & Machine Learning

Amrita Vishwa Vidyapeetham  ·  2024–2025  ·  Team lead  ·  4 members  ·  2 IEEE conference papers

Physically testing motors with precisely controlled demagnetization levels — 5%, 10%, 15%, 20%, 25% — was not practically feasible. Our approach was to generate controlled fault conditions through high-fidelity FEM simulation (ANSYS Motor-CAD), using the actual motor geometry and material specifications to produce training datasets that carried real physical meaning rather than being synthetic approximations.

I led a team of four: coordinating the modelling pipeline from fault parameterisation and simulation through feature extraction and model selection. The final system used Gradient Boosting classifiers trained on FEM-generated data, achieving robust fault-severity discrimination across all demagnetization classes. I presented both resulting papers at their respective IEEE conferences.

Two IEEE conference papers. What this project developed: building research infrastructure from zero, generating reliable datasets where none exist, making engineering decisions under uncertainty, leading a technical team, and producing fully documented and reproducible work. These are the same capabilities required in fabrication research.
